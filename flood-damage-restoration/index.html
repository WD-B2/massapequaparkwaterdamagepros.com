Flood damage cleanup starts with getting people out of the water.

Flood damage cleanup in Massapequa Park, NY starts with getting people and pets out of the water and keeping them out. The 24/7 line is open tonight. While you wait for a crew, shut off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water, lift valuables to higher shelves and photograph everything. Do not wade near outlets, a furnace or a water heater.

Flood damage means water that came from outside or from a source you cannot stop at a fixture. Storm surge, overflowing drains, groundwater rising through a slab and a backed-up creek all count. It differs from a burst pipe because floodwater often carries soil, sewage and chemicals, so restoration crews treat it as contaminated and follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration.

Public records document street flooding in the lower streets of the village, and the Philadelphia Avenue Area page lays out what they say.

When floodwater is an emergency.

Call before you start cleaning if any of these are true.

  • Water touches outlets, appliances or the electrical panel. Stay out. Call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk before you call us..
  • The water is dark or smells like sewage. Do not touch it with bare skin and keep children and pets away from it..
  • Water is still rising. Move to higher ground first. Extraction cannot begin until the source recedes or is stopped..
  • The water has sat for a day or more. Carpet pad, insulation and drywall start to fail, and mold needs only moisture and time..

Nothing on this page replaces emergency services. If anyone is in danger from water or power, call 911 first.

Why South Shore flooding behaves differently.

Massapequa Park sits on Long Island's South Shore in Nassau County, close to the bays. Superstorm Sandy in 2012 showed how tidal surge and storm rain can overwhelm drainage here. In January 2024 the Town of Oyster Bay finished a federally funded flood diversion project on low-lying streets in nearby southern Massapequa, adding underground retention and tidal check valves. A project like that helps the streets it covers, but it does not stop water from entering your house.

Whether your address sits in a mapped flood zone is something to look up yourself, so check the FEMA flood map for your address. Then ask the Village of Massapequa Park Building Department about permits before rebuilding below a mapped flood elevation. From pumping to a verified dry house.

Most flood jobs follow the same order, even when the rooms and materials differ.

  1. 1Make the area safe.The crew checks for electrical hazards, gas and weak floors, then sets up containment so contaminated air and debris stay put..
  2. 2Extract standing water.Submersible pumps and extraction units remove what you can see, as described on the water extraction page..
  3. 3Remove what cannot be saved.Soaked pad, wet insulation and swollen drywall come out. Items touched by Category 3 water are usually discarded, and the crew photographs and lists them for your claim..
  4. 4Clean and treat.Hard surfaces are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial product suited to the material..
  5. 5Dry and verify.Air movers and dehumidifiers run while moisture meters track progress, as covered under structural drying. Drying ends when readings match dry reference materials, not when a room feels dry..

Mold can start when materials stay damp, so some flood jobs end with mold remediation if growth is found. Not every flood leads there.

Flood damage and your insurance policy.

Standard homeowners policies often cover sudden and accidental water, such as a burst pipe, but damage from rising water is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer which one applies to your loss.

Crews document the loss with photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope so you have records to share with your adjuster. No one can promise how a claim will turn out, so keep your own notes as well.

Ask any restoration company whether its crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for professional water damage restoration. What to do first: stop the source if it is safe to do so, keep clear of wet electrical equipment and call the 24/7 line.
