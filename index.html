Water damage restoration for Massapequa Park homes and businesses.

Water damage restoration in Massapequa Park, NY means removing water fast, drying what can be saved and documenting the loss. Our 24/7 line takes calls at any hour. The village sits on Long Island's South Shore in Nassau County, near the bays, so homes here deal with storm water, aging plumbing and basements that take on water.

Clean water from a burst pipe is easiest to handle if it is removed within a day. Contaminated water from a storm, a flood or a sewer backup needs more care and protective equipment. Crews follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which classifies water by how contaminated it is and sets out drying principles.

Not every job needs a full crew. A small appliance spill may only need extraction and a day of drying, while a flood can take a week or more. Dispatch asks a few questions and explains which situation you are in.

What to do in the first minutes.

These steps are safe for most homes.

  • Stop the water. Close the fixture shutoff or the main valve..
  • Stay clear of electricity. Do not touch outlets or panels near water..
  • Lift belongings. Move rugs, shoes and electronics off the floor..
  • Photograph the damage. Record it before you move anything..
  • Call the 24/7 line. Describe what you see and where it started..

For storm water see storm water damage cleanup. For a flooded basement see basement water damage cleanup.

How the source of the water changes the job.

Clean supply water is the most forgiving. A failed line, a washer hose or a water heater tank usually releases clean water, and carpet, pad and drywall may be saved if they are dried quickly. A sewer backup is the opposite, and porous materials that touch it are removed. Floodwater and storm surge sit in between and are often treated as contaminated.

Hidden water is the other variable. Water that runs inside a wall or under a floor can be wet for days before it shows. That is why crews use moisture meters and thermal cameras, and why drying is monitored until readings match unaffected materials.
