Seasonal Water Damage Risks in Massapequa Park, NY.

Water damage follows the calendar. Each season brings its own causes, and a short seasonal check can prevent many of them.

How the seasons change the risk.

Long Island's South Shore has four distinct seasons, and each stresses plumbing, roofs and drainage differently. Winter freezes pipes, spring rain finds weak spots, summer humidity and air conditioning produce condensation and fall storms bring wind and surge.

Knowing the pattern helps you inspect the right things at the right time. A ten-minute seasonal check is cheaper than a cleanup.

Why coastal weather is harder on houses.

Salt in the air speeds corrosion on metal fittings, flashings and window hardware. Humid summers keep indoor air moist and encourage mildew. Nor'easters and tropical systems bring wind that pushes rain sideways, into places that stay dry in ordinary storms.

Even without a hurricane, a South Shore house sees many wet days a year. That is why small maintenance jobs, like caulking and flashing repair, are worth doing before they turn into ceiling stains.

What to do in the first cold snap.

The first hard freeze of the year catches many homeowners off guard. Hoses left on outdoor spigots hold water that expands and splits the pipe behind the wall. Disconnect hoses in the fall, close the interior shutoff to outdoor faucets if you have one and open the spigot to let the line drain.

Garages, crawl spaces and exterior walls are the other weak points. If a room feels much colder than the rest of the house, the pipes in or near it are at risk. Insulation sleeves cost little. A licensed plumber can tell you whether a vulnerable run should be rerouted.

Winter and early spring.

Cold and thaw cycles.

Frozen and split pipes.

Pipes in exterior walls, garages and crawl spaces are most at risk. Keep the heat on and let faucets drip during extreme cold.

Ice dams.

Ice at the roof edge blocks melting snow, which backs up under shingles and into the ceiling. Insulation and ventilation reduce the risk.

Summer and fall.

Heat, humidity and storms.

Air conditioner overflow.

Clogged condensate lines overflow in summer. Have the system serviced before the hot season.

Thunderstorms and tropical storms.

Wind-driven rain and surge arrive in late summer and fall. See storm water damage cleanup for what happens after.

A simple year-round habit list.

These cost little and catch problems early.

  • Look under sinks and behind appliances every few months..
  • Check the water meter with everything off to see if it moves..
  • Clean gutters in spring and fall..
  • Test the sump pump each spring..
  • Replace washer hoses every few years..

Summer storms and humid months.

Thunderstorms in summer can drop heavy rain in short bursts, and clogged gutters overflow against siding and foundations. Walk the yard after a storm and look for pooling near the house. Downspouts that dump water at the foundation are a leading cause of wet basements.

Air conditioners are working hard in July and August. Check the condensate line for dripping, look at the ceiling near any attic unit and replace filters on schedule. A dehumidifier in the basement keeps humidity in check, and a quick glance at it each week is a cheap early warning.

Keep an eye on bathroom and kitchen fans too, since steam from summer showers and cooking adds to indoor humidity that is already high.

A twice-a-year inspection.

Spring and fall are good times to walk the property.

Outside.

Look at roof edges, flashing around chimneys and vents, gutters, downspouts, siding gaps and the soil slope near the foundation.

Inside.

Check ceilings and the tops of walls for stains. Look under sinks and behind appliances. Smell the basement.

Utilities.

Test the sump pump, look at the water heater for rust and exercise the main shutoff.

When a seasonal problem becomes an emergency.

A damp spot becomes an emergency when water is running, when it touches electrical equipment or when it spreads to more than one room. At that point stop the source if you can and call. The water extraction page explains what the crew does first.

Winter and storm seasons raise call volume, so early calls and early photos matter.

What to do after any wet season.

When a stormy or freezing stretch ends, take a short tour of the house. Look at ceilings, check the basement floor, smell the closets and look at the outside of the foundation. Small stains caught in a week are cheap to fix and prove when the damage began.

Keeping a home log.

A simple notebook or note on your phone helps. Record the dates you cleaned gutters, tested the sump pump, replaced hoses and serviced the AC. If a loss happens, those entries show an insurer that you maintained the home.
