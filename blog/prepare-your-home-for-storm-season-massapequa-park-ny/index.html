Prepare Your Massapequa Park Home for Storm Season.

A few hours of preparation before a storm can limit water damage. This checklist focuses on what matters on Long Island's South Shore.

Why South Shore homes need a plan.

Massapequa Park sits on Long Island's South Shore, close to bays and tidal waters. Superstorm Sandy in 2012 showed how tidal surge and heavy rain can combine, and smaller storms still push wind-driven rain through gaps. A written plan takes the guesswork out of a stressful night.

Look up whether your address is in a mapped flood zone on the FEMA flood map, and ask the Village of Massapequa Park or the Town of Oyster Bay about local emergency guidance. Local officials publish evacuation and alert information that no general article can replace.

Outside the house.

Do these before the season starts.

  • Clean gutters and downspouts and extend downspouts away from the foundation..
  • Check the roof for loose shingles, cracked flashing and worn vent boots..
  • Seal gaps around windows, doors and utility penetrations..
  • Look at grading to confirm the ground slopes away from the house..
  • Secure outdoor items that could blow into windows..

A one-page family plan.

Write down where the main water shutoff, the electrical panel and the gas shutoff are. Add the phone numbers for your insurer, your utility and your restoration contact. Tape the page inside a cabinet and put a copy in your phone. Under stress, a written plan beats memory.

Decide where you will go if you have to leave, and who will check on elderly neighbors. Keep chargers, flashlights, medications and copies of documents in one bag. Local officials announce evacuation zones, so know yours before the season begins.

Inside the house.

Do these in the days before a forecast storm.

  • Test the sump pump by pouring water into the pit, and consider a battery backup..
  • Locate the main water shutoff and the electrical panel, and make sure everyone knows where they are..
  • Move valuables off basement floors and into waterproof containers..
  • Charge phones and keep a flashlight handy..
  • Photograph rooms and belongings for your records..

Gutters, grading and downspouts in detail.

Gutters carry roof water away. When they clog with leaves, the water spills over the edge and lands beside the foundation. Clean them in spring and fall, check that joints are sealed and make sure each downspout empties several feet from the house, onto ground that slopes away.

Grading matters just as much. Soil should fall away from the foundation by about six inches over the first ten feet where possible. If you see settling, add compacted fill rather than loose mulch, and keep flower beds from holding water against the wall.

Window wells need covers and working drains. A clogged window well can fill like a bucket and push water in through the glass.

Insurance checks.

Do this before the storm, not after.

Check.Why.
Flood insurance.Standard homeowners policies exclude rising water.
Water backup endorsement.Sewer and sump failure are often excluded without it.
Windstorm deductible.Some policies set a separate deductible.
Contents coverage.Confirm what is included.
Policy documents.Keep copies somewhere safe.

Flood policies usually have a waiting period before they take effect, so ask your agent early.

If water gets in anyway.

Stay safe first. Do not wade through water near electrical equipment, and avoid driving through flooded roads. Once it is safe, photograph the damage and call a restoration crew. Pages on storm water damage, flood damage and basement water damage explain what the cleanup involves.

Do not return to an evacuated home until officials say it is safe.

After the storm.

Walk the exterior for damage, check the roof from the ground and look for water stains inside. Run a dehumidifier in damp rooms and keep records of everything. Call your insurer promptly to report the loss.

Storm demand can be high in the days after a major event, so early documentation and early calls both help.

Protecting the basement.

Basements take the first hit when water rises.

Raise what you can.

Put boxes on shelves or pallets, not directly on the floor. Move electronics and papers upstairs. Keep the water heater and furnace elevated if possible.

Check drains.

Floor drains should flow freely. A backflow valve, installed by a licensed plumber, can keep sewage from coming back up a drain during heavy rain.

Have a pump plan.

A battery backup sump pump keeps working when the power fails. A portable pump and hose can help in a pinch.

Documents and photos to prepare now.

Before storm season, walk through each room with a phone and record a video of belongings, appliances and finishes. Open drawers and closets. Narrate model numbers when you can. Store the video in cloud storage so a flood in the house does not take the record with it.

Gather insurance policies, the declarations page, photos of the exterior and any receipts for major purchases. If you rent, do the same for your belongings and confirm that your renters policy is active.

For reference, ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for professional water damage restoration, and S520 covers mold remediation. When to call: if water is active or spreading, or if you are unsure whether materials are dry, call the 24/7 line.
