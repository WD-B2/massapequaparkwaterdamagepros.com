Wet Mattress or Sofa After a Leak: Save It or Toss It?.

Upholstered furniture and mattresses hold water deep inside. The water type and the time wet usually decide the answer.

The two questions that decide it.

Whether a wet mattress or sofa can be saved depends on what kind of water it was and how long it stayed wet. Clean water from a supply line, caught within a day or so, gives you a chance. Contaminated water from a flood or sewer backup, or anything wet for several days, usually means the item should go.

Foam, batting and padding hold water in their core and dry slowly. Surface drying does not reach the inside, which is why mold and odor can develop in furniture that looks dry.

Save or discard.

General guidance.

Item and situation.Usual outcome.Why.
Mattress, clean water, wet less than a day.May be dried.Some chance to dry the core.
Mattress, wet several days.Usually discarded.Mold and odor in the core.
Sofa, clean water, dried fast.May be saved.Frame can dry, cushions may need cleaning.
Upholstery touched by sewage or floodwater.Discard.Contamination cannot be removed from porous material.
Solid wood or metal frames.Often saved.Can be dried and cleaned.
Leather.Case by case.Can warp and stain.

Why foam and fabric are hard to dry.

A mattress contains foam, springs, batting and layers of fabric, all of which can hold water. Water moves into the core and then evaporates slowly, so the outside can feel dry while the middle is still damp. The same is true of cushions and thick upholstery.

Wet fabric also traps soil and bacteria. If the water was not clean, or if the item stayed wet for more than a day or two, the risk of odor, mold and skin irritation rises. This is why professionals are more cautious about soft furnishings than about hard surfaces.

What to do right away.

Act fast on clean-water spills.

  1. 1Move it off the wet area.Place it on foil, plastic or blocks so it does not wick more water..
  2. 2Photograph it.Record the item before you do anything..
  3. 3Blot and extract.Press with towels. A wet vac can help on a clean spill..
  4. 4Dry with airflow.Fans and a dehumidifier help, and sunlight outdoors if the weather allows..
  5. 5Reassess in a day or two.If it still smells or feels damp inside, discard it..

Safety and health.

Wear gloves when handling anything that touched contaminated water. Do not sleep on or sit on a mattress or sofa that stayed wet or smells musty, because mold can irritate lungs and skin. If anyone in the home has asthma or allergies, be more cautious.

Disposal rules for mattresses vary by locality. Ask your sanitation department how to dispose of a large item before leaving it at the curb.

Insurance and documentation.

Contents are often covered under homeowners and renters policies when a sudden covered loss damages them. Keep photos, receipts or product details and a list of what you discarded. Do not throw items out before documenting them, and ask your insurer whether the adjuster needs to see them first.

For the floor and walls near the furniture, see carpet water damage and water extraction for how the area is dried.

Replacing versus restoring contents.

For furniture with sentimental value, ask a professional upholstery cleaner whether the piece can be saved. Solid wood frames often survive well and can be refinished. Upholstery may be re-covered. The cost is sometimes close to replacement, so ask for a quote before deciding.

For items covered by insurance, ask the insurer whether they prefer to see the item before disposal. Take photos of tags and labels. A sofa that stays wet for days can also stain the floor beneath it, so check the carpet and subfloor under any piece you remove.

What about other contents.

Different items follow different rules.

Books and papers.

Paper can be frozen to stop mold and sent to a document restoration service. Do it quickly.

Electronics.

Do not plug in anything that got wet. A technician can often recover data if you leave the device off.

Clothing and linens.

Washable items can often be saved if they are cleaned promptly. Dry-clean-only items may need a professional cleaner.

When the whole room is wet.

If the leak soaked more than a few items, ask a restoration crew to assess the room. A moisture meter finds water in the floor and walls, and the crew can advise on contents. Contents cleaning is a separate service from structural drying and may be handled by a different company.

The structural drying page explains how the building is dried while contents are sorted.

Protecting the floor under furniture.

Wet furniture left on carpet or wood stains the floor and keeps it damp. Lift it onto blocks or foil as soon as you can, and check the underside for water marks. If a stain appears on a wood floor, tell the crew so they can check the subfloor.

Kids, pets and allergies.

If the household includes small children, pets or anyone with asthma, be stricter. Discard items sooner and keep them out of rooms where drying equipment runs. Wash bedding on hot and replace pillows that were near the water.

For reference, ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for professional water damage restoration, and S520 covers mold remediation. When to call: if water is active or spreading, or if you are unsure whether materials are dry, call the 24/7 line.
