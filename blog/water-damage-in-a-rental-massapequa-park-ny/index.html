Water Damage in a Rental in Massapequa Park, NY: What Tenants Should Do.

When water hits a rental, the first hour is about safety and records. Who pays for what comes later, and it depends on your lease and the cause.

Start with safety, then tell the landlord.

If water is entering your rental, protect people first. Stay out of rooms where water touches outlets, keep children and pets away and shut off the fixture valve if it is under your sink, behind a toilet or at an appliance. If you cannot stop the water, call the landlord or building manager right away.

Tell them in writing as well, even if you also spoke by phone. A text or email creates a timestamp. Include what you saw, when you noticed it and what you did, and attach photos.

What to document.

Keep these records.

  • Photos and video of every wet surface, the source and your damaged belongings..
  • Dates and times of when you first noticed it and when you reported it..
  • All messages with the landlord, building manager and insurer..
  • Receipts for emergency purchases or temporary lodging..
  • A list of damaged items with approximate value and age..

Who usually handles what.

Leases and local law decide the details, so this is a general guide.

Ask the landlord who will arrange drying. If the building owner uses a restoration company, ask for the scope in writing.

Writing the notice to your landlord.

A clear notice makes everything that follows easier. State the date and time you found the water, the exact location, what you think the source is and what you have already done, such as closing a valve. Attach photos and say plainly that you expect the problem to be repaired and the area dried.

Send it by a method that leaves a record, such as email or text, and keep a copy. If your lease requires a particular form of notice, follow it as well. Written notice also matters later if the damage gets worse because repairs were delayed.

Renters insurance.

A landlord's policy covers the building, not your belongings. Renters insurance typically covers your personal property from sudden covered losses and may include additional living expenses if you cannot stay in the unit. Flood is usually excluded, and sewer backup often needs an endorsement.

If you do not have renters insurance, document everything anyway. Landlords and their insurers may still be responsible for the building damage, but your belongings may not be covered by anyone else.

Why drying cannot wait.

Water spreads into walls and floors and can move between units. Mold may begin to grow within a couple of days. A tenant has a good reason to press for fast drying, because delays affect health and belongings. Ask the landlord how and when drying will start, and document any delay.

If your unit is part of a building with other units, the property manager is usually the right call for coordinating. The commercial water damage page covers multi-unit building losses, and structural drying explains how dryness is verified.

If the landlord does not respond.

Keep sending written notices. Check your lease for repair procedures and your local housing office for tenant rights. New York has strong tenant protections, and a local tenant organization or legal aid service can explain what applies to you. This page is general information and not legal advice.

In an emergency such as flooding or sewage, you can still call a restoration company for immediate help, but tell the landlord first where possible, because the building owner usually controls repairs.

Know your local resources.

Tenants in Nassau County can contact local housing and code enforcement offices if the landlord does not act. Legal aid organizations and tenant groups on Long Island can explain rights in plain language.

This page is general information and not legal advice. If the issue is serious or the landlord is unresponsive, speak with a qualified professional.

Staying in the unit or leaving.

Safety and comfort decide this.

When to leave.

Leave if the water touches electrical equipment, if there is sewage or if the ceiling sags. Stay with family or in a hotel and keep receipts.

When you can stay.

A clean, limited leak that has been stopped is often livable while drying runs. Expect noisy equipment for several days.

Who covers temporary housing.

That depends on the lease, the cause of the damage and your renters policy. Ask both your landlord and your insurer before spending large sums.

For reference, ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for professional water damage restoration, and S520 covers mold remediation. When to call: if water is active or spreading, or if you are unsure whether materials are dry, call the 24/7 line.
