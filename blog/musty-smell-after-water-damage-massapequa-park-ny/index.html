Musty Smell After Water Damage in Massapequa Park, NY.

A musty smell after a leak is a signal. It usually means moisture is still trapped somewhere you cannot see.

What the smell is telling you.

A musty or earthy smell comes from microbial growth, usually mold or bacteria, feeding on damp material. When you smell it after a leak, flood or spill, something in the structure is still wet or was wet long enough for growth to start. Air fresheners cover the smell without fixing the cause.

The smell is often stronger in the morning, in humid weather or when the heating or cooling comes on. Those are clues about where air and moisture are moving.

Where the smell hides.

Look at these places first.

Location.Why it holds moisture.How to check.
Under carpet and pad.Pad is a sponge.Lift a corner and feel or smell.
Inside walls.Drywall and insulation hold water.Moisture meter.
Under cabinets.Leaks run behind toe kicks.Remove the toe kick and look.
Basements and crawl spaces.Concrete and soil stay damp.Hygrometer and meter.

Smells that are not mold.

A damp smell is not always mold. Sewer gas from a dry trap smells like rotten eggs and goes away when the trap is refilled. Wet dog or stale cigarette smells can live in carpet and pad. Mildew on towels or a clogged washing machine can fill a laundry room with a sour odor.

The important question is whether the smell is tied to moisture. If it gets stronger after rain, near a particular wall or when the humidity rises, treat it as a moisture problem until a meter shows otherwise. If it is the same in every season, a different source is more likely.

How to track it down.

Use this order.

  1. 1Find the strongest spot.Walk the house and note where the smell peaks..
  2. 2Look for stains or soft spots.Check ceilings, wall bases and flooring..
  3. 3Use a meter.A moisture meter reads wood and drywall. A thermal camera shows cool, wet areas..
  4. 4Open a small inspection area.A crew can open a small section of wall or lift flooring..
  5. 5Dry and clean.Dry the structure and remove what cannot be saved. See structural drying..

Why drying has to come first.

Cleaning a musty area without drying it only works until the moisture feeds the growth again. Dehumidifiers and air movers lower moisture in the structure, and meter readings confirm the result. Once dry, remaining odor from dead growth can be removed by cleaning and, if needed, removing affected material.

If you find visible growth, do not disturb it. See mold remediation for New York licensing rules, and keep the area closed off while you decide.

What not to do.

These make it worse.

  • Do not rely on air fresheners or ozone machines, which cover odor and do not dry anything..
  • Do not keep running the heating or cooling if the ducts may be contaminated..
  • Do not scrub large areas of mold without protection..
  • Do not seal damp areas with paint. Moisture will blister it..
  • Do not ignore it for weeks. Growth spreads..

Humidity as a clue.

A cheap hygrometer helps.

Normal range.

Indoor relative humidity between about 30 and 50 percent is comfortable for most homes. Above 60 percent for long periods encourages growth.

Where to measure.

Check a basement, a bathroom and a bedroom. Compare readings in the morning and evening.

What to do.

Run a dehumidifier in damp rooms and make sure fans vent outdoors. If readings stay high after that, look for a hidden leak.

When the smell is in the air handler.

A musty smell that appears only when the heating or cooling runs points to the HVAC system. Condensate can pool in the drain pan, and damp filters or coils can host growth. The duct system itself can pick up moisture in an attic or crawl space.

Turn the system off and call an HVAC technician. Do not keep running a system you suspect is contaminated. A restoration crew can dry the surrounding structure, and the technician can clean or replace the components. See the page on AC leak water damage for related damage.

Changing the filter is the simplest first step. A clogged filter restricts airflow, which can cause freezing and condensate overflow.

A smell after a recent leak.

If the smell started within days of a leak, the fix may be simple, such as extracting water and drying a pad or lower wall. See carpet water damage and wall and drywall water damage for those cases.

If it started long after, a slow hidden leak is more likely, and a plumber or restoration crew with a meter can locate it.

When to call right away.

Call promptly if the smell comes with visible growth, if anyone in the home has new breathing symptoms or if a recent flood left materials wet for more than a couple of days. These situations call for fast drying and, in some cases, a licensed assessor.

For reference, ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for professional water damage restoration, and S520 covers mold remediation. When to call: if water is active or spreading, or if you are unsure whether materials are dry, call the 24/7 line.
