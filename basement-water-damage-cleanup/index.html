Basement water damage in Massapequa Park, NY: first steps.

Basement water damage in Massapequa Park, NY is urgent because the furnace, water heater, electrical panel and stored belongings often sit at the lowest point of the house. The 24/7 line is open now. Before you step into any standing water, look from the top of the stairs for outlets, appliances and the panel, and if any are touched by water, stay out and call the utility or 911.

If your home has a basement, it is the first place water goes when a sump pump fails, a supply line breaks upstairs or heavy rain overwhelms drainage. Whether a particular street is prone to seepage is a question for the building department and your neighbors, so no general local claim is made here.

When a wet basement is an emergency.

Call right away for any of these.

  • Water covers the floor and is still rising. Keep out and call. The source may be a failed pump, a broken line or groundwater..
  • The furnace, water heater or panel stands in water. Do not touch them. Gas and electrical risks come before restoration..
  • The water smells like sewage. Floor drains can back up, and the water is then contaminated. See the sewage backup page..
  • A finished basement has carpet, drywall or insulation that is soaked. These materials hold water against framing and start to fail within a day or two..

Why basements stay wet long after the water is gone.

Below-grade rooms dry slowly for reasons that have nothing to do with how careful anyone was.

Concrete holds water.

Poured concrete and block walls absorb moisture and release it slowly, so a floor that looks dry on top can read wet on a meter for days.

Air does not move.

Basements have few windows and little airflow. Dehumidifiers do most of the work there, and they run longer than in an upstairs room.

Finishes trap damp.

Carpet, wall paneling and insulation against a masonry wall hide moisture. Removing the bottom of drywall is often the only way to dry what sits behind it.

Insurance and a wet basement.

Water from a sudden event such as a burst line is often covered, while seepage, groundwater and neglect often are not, and flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Sump pump failure and sewer backup are often excluded unless an endorsement was added, so check your policy or ask your insurer.

Photograph the water level and every damaged item before moving anything. The crew adds moisture readings and a written scope for your adjuster.

Ask any restoration company whether its crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for professional water damage restoration. What to do first: stop the source if it is safe to do so, keep clear of wet electrical equipment and call the 24/7 line.
