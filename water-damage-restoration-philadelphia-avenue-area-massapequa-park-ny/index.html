Where this area is.

The Philadelphia Avenue Area is not a marketing district. It is the name an engineering firm used for a stormwater project in the Village of Massapequa Park, NY. A 2011 local news report described Atlantic, Ocean, Philadelphia and Eastlake avenues, between Pennsylvania Avenue and Front Street, as the lowest part of the village.

This page uses that area as its subject because the public record there is unusually clear. It does not claim an official boundary. If your street is nearby, the same advice applies, and dispatch can confirm coverage from your address.

Regional storms are not street conditions.

The National Weather Service recorded Hurricane Sandy's surge on October 29, 2012 at about 7.8 feet in Reynolds Channel and 8.1 feet at East Rockaway, and its local reports for that day include a high water rescue in Massapequa. Those are regional records.

They do not say whether any street in the village flooded that night, so this page does not claim that. Keep storm history and street history separate when you read about your own block.

First steps when water enters a low-lying home.

Water from the street or a backed-up drain is not clean water. Use these steps while you wait for help.

  • Do not stand in water near electricity. If the panel, an outlet or a cord is wet, stay back, leave the breakers alone and call the utility..
  • Treat street and drain water as contaminated. Keep children and pets out, wear waterproof boots and gloves, and wash hands after contact..
  • Leave sewage to professionals. If a toilet or floor drain has backed up, do not try to mop, vacuum or dry it yourself..
  • Move belongings up. Get small items, papers and electronics onto shelves or counters..
  • Photograph the waterline inside and outside, then call. The pictures show how deep the water got and where it came from..

Basements after heavy rain.

In a low part of a village, the basement is usually where water shows up first. Window wells, floor drains and sump pits are the usual entry points, and a pump that fails or cannot keep up leaves water standing on the floor.

Standing water has to leave before drying can start. See basement water damage cleanup for how a wet basement is handled, and flood damage restoration if the water came from outside. Carpet and pad that soaked in dirty water are usually removed rather than dried.

Insurance and flood coverage.

A standard homeowners policy often excludes flood, meaning water that rises from outside. Backup of a sewer or drain is a separate question and often needs an added endorsement. The county plan describes the village as in good standing with the National Flood Insurance Program, which says nothing about any single address.

Ask your agent what is covered, whether flood coverage has a waiting period and what to document. The crew provides photos, moisture readings and a written scope. Only your insurer decides coverage.
