Carpet water damage: what can be saved.

Carpet water damage in Massapequa Park, NY is judged by two questions: where did the water come from and how long has it been wet. Clean water from a supply line or appliance that is extracted within a day or two can often leave the carpet in place. Dirty water or long wetting usually means the carpet or pad must come out. The 24/7 line is open now.

The pad is the sponge. It holds water against the carpet backing and the subfloor, and it dries slowly. Many crews remove and replace the pad even when the carpet is saved, because pad is inexpensive compared with the risk of leaving it damp.

What to do before the crew arrives.

These steps reduce the damage.

  • Stop the source. Close the fixture shutoff or the main valve..
  • Remove furniture and place foil under legs. Wood and metal stains transfer to wet carpet..
  • Pick up small rugs and wet items. Hang them to dry or set them aside..
  • Do not use a household vacuum on wet carpet. It is unsafe and does not remove water from the pad..
  • Open windows if the weather allows. Airflow helps, but it is not a substitute for extraction..

The decision follows the water category and the time wet.

How wet carpet is treated.

The method depends on whether the carpet stays or goes.

  1. 1Extract water.A weighted wand pulls water out of carpet and pad. See water extraction..
  2. 2Lift and inspect.Carpet is peeled back from the tack strip so the pad and subfloor can be checked..
  3. 3Remove the pad if needed.Wet pad is cut out and disposed of..
  4. 4Dry the carpet and subfloor.Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings reach target. See structural drying..
  5. 5Reinstall or replace.Saved carpet is stretched and laid on new pad. Otherwise a flooring installer fits new carpet..

Carpet and insurance.

Sudden water from a supply line or appliance often falls under covered losses, while long-term leaks may not. Check your policy or ask your insurer how carpet is treated.

Keep a sample of the carpet if it is replaced, because it can help match a new piece and document the original material.

Ask any restoration company whether its crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for professional water damage restoration. What to do first: stop the source if it is safe to do so, keep clear of wet electrical equipment and call the 24/7 line.
